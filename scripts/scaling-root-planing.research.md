# Research: scaling and root planing (2026-11-01)

## Facts (used in script)
- 47.2% of US adults aged 30+ had periodontitis in 2009-2010 (rounded to "about 47%" in the report's introduction).
  CDC MMWR: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/su6203a21.htm
- SRP is a deep cleaning that removes tartar and bacteria around the tooth roots. Scaling removes plaque and tartar;
  root planing smooths the roots to keep plaque and tartar from reattaching. Usually more than one appointment to treat
  all teeth. Temporary discomfort while healing; overall risks minimal.
  Cleveland Clinic: https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23983-tooth-scaling-and-root-planing
- Deep cleaning targets plaque and tartar below the gum line; root planing follows scaling.
  Colgate: https://www.colgate.com/en-ph/oral-health/dental-visits/deep-cleaning-your-teeth-when-to-do-it-0314
- Usually done under local anaesthetic; NHS clinic splits the mouth into four parts, one per visit.
  Kent Community Health NHS leaflet: https://www.kentcht.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/Scale-and-root-planing-00716-v7.pdf
- Minor discomfort and sensitivity afterwards is normal and should not last longer than a few days (as reported in search
  summaries of Oral-B's explainer; not fetched directly): https://oralb.com/en-us/oral-health/conditions/tartar-plaque/dental-scaling-and-root-planing-explained
- Healthy gum tissue does not normally bleed during routine brushing; regular bleeding is a reason to see a dentist
  (ADA guidance as relayed by Colgate): https://www.colgate.com/en-us/oral-health/gum-disease/bleeding-gums-when-brushing
